About me

As a software developer turned UX researcher, I focus on understanding how people interact with technology and translating those insights into product decisions. My work has spanned healthcare, online privacy, digital and community wellbeing, and education, using both qualitative and quantitative methods to study complex human experiences and inform design decisions.

Currently, I am researching how users understand privacy labels and the relationship between recreational sport and mental wellbeing. Previously, I conducted research on children’s technology use, digital nudging, healthcare applications, and privacy, including published work on how different generations understand privacy terminology. I enjoy moving between interviews, thematic analysis, survey design, quantitative analysis, and data storytelling to uncover patterns that lead to meaningful improvements.

Before transitioning into UX research, I worked in software development, building internal tools and collaborating with technical teams across industries including retail, technology, and sports. My software background helps me bridge research, design, and engineering, making findings actionable and grounded in real-world constraints.

Outside of work, I can usually be found reading historical fiction, walking in nature, or trying to convince friends to play another board game.
