Teacher Perspectives on Children’s Technology Usage and Online Privacy & Security

Charlotte Moremen & Eleanor Birrell

Poster · Symposium on Usable Privacy & Security (SOUPS), 2026

Read the extended abstract

We interviewed 24 full-time K–12 classroom teachers across the United States about how their students use technology in class, for homework, and at leisure, and about who, if anyone, is responsible for teaching kids to navigate it safely. Most teachers we spoke with believed their students face real privacy and security risks, ranging from excessive surveillance by authority figures to real-world security threats such as school shootings and sex trafficking. Additionally, privacy and security education, where it existed at all, was left almost entirely to individual teachers' discretion, with no shared standard for what students should know or when. Several teachers pointed out that this leaves an open question with no clear owner — schools, parents, platforms, or regulators — and our results suggest that current EdTech tools are rarely evaluated for their privacy risks to students, only for their educational value.
